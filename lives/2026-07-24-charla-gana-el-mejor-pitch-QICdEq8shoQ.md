## The core pitch formula — timing and structure

Ángela proposes a rigid time-boxed framework for a 2-minute recorded/live pitch:
- **Hook or attention-grabbing question + problem statement**: 20 seconds. `"Empiecen con un hook o algo que llame la atención y explicando el problema. 20 segundos."` The explicit anti-pattern is starting with self-introduction: `"La mayoría de las personas comienzan diciendo, 'Hola, me llamo Ángela y soy de Venezuela y vivo en Colombia y entonces yo participo en esta hackaton. 20 minutos gun, 20 segundos gun, se fueron.'"` Instead, open with data or analogy connecting to a real problem. Example given: `"en Colombia en 2025 cerca de 4.8 millones de hogares se quedaron en déficit de vivienda. ¿Cuántos de ellos califican para un subsidio y nunca lo reclaman?"` — the framing pre-loads urgency and relevance before naming the solution.
- **"What did you build?" description**: 20 seconds. `"Describe el producto en una frase. ¿Qué lo hace distinto? el caso de uso central y no la lista de features."` Phrase it as: *[product name]. In X questions it tells you Y and generates Z.* For the reto-4 analogy she offers: `"Construimos mi subsidio. En tres preguntas te dice a qué subsidio calificas y te arma una solicitud."` The mental model: **one specific outcome, not feature list.**
- **Live demo or recorded screen walkthrough**: 50 seconds. `"Graben solo ese paso en particular en el que se quieren enfocar... Enseña el flujo real, responde las tres preguntas, match de subsidios, solicitud generada, pum, sigo."` Key: if submission is via pre-recorded video, **back up your screen recording because WiFi may fail**. She recommends: `"Si no sé cómo funcionan las submissions... si es submitir a un form, entonces graben el backup. E si si tienen que presentarlo en vivo, graben el backup por si fai y el wifi."` (i.e., always have a MP4 backup). For live finals, practitioners *must* **rehearse the step-by-step flow so deeply that they can defend it to judges even if the live demo fails.**
- **Tech stack / "why this matters"**: 10 seconds. `"el ah stack tecnológico o el cumo de las herramientas que usaron o por qué usaron esas herramientas y por qué importa."` Do not list tools; explain **why these tools are relevant to the sponsor** (in our case, Colsubsidio). The framing: *We chose [tech] because it solves [sponsor's specific pain].*
- **Team introduction + closing call-to-action**: 20 seconds. `"El equipo que sigue el cierre 20 segundos... Somos Pepito y María. Uno construye, la otra vende..."` and close with a strong, future-tense ask: `"El siguiente paso es un piloto de 3 meses con subsidio si somos finalistas. Si funciona con sus afiliados, funciona para millones de colombianos..."` This frames continued engagement with the sponsor.

**Total: 20 + 20 + 50 + 10 + 20 = 120 seconds (2 minutes).**

## What judges reward and penalize

**Reward (explicit from Ángela's experience as a judge)**:
- **Problem clarity in the first 20 seconds.** `"Si en los primeros 20 segundos hablaste de ti, estás en problemas."` If judges do not understand the problem by the 20-second mark, you've already lost attentional bandwidth.
- **Explaining *how* you solved the problem, not just *that* you solved it.** `"La mayoría de las personas con ella hoy me dicen, no sé [when asked 'how did you solve X?']. Entonces, por ejemplo, en la parte de Textac, yo les recomendaría un montón que traten de incluir cómo resolvieron el problema. Eh, y eso a mí siempre dice, 'Ah, esta persona sabe lo que está haciendo, sabe eh lo planeó, sabe cómo presentarlo...'"` Judges ask technical questions during the 3-min Q&A; teams that have thought through *why* they chose their approach sound prepared and trustworthy.
- **A "wow" factor—but defined narrowly.** `"Para tomar en cuenta si queremos generar un efecto wow... muchas personas, lo puedo prometer, comienzan diciendo, 'Hola, me llamo Ángela...' y son como 30 segundos en que yo estoy como, Dios mío, arranca."` Wow = hooking the judge so hard they stop multi-tasking. In her words: `"Eso está muy buena. Esta pregunta es una gran pregunta... ¿Qué hace que un equipo sea diferencial del otro? que tenga una estructura la la presentación y que sepa explicarme cuando yo pregunto cosas."` Wow is *structure + preparation + ability to explain*, not flashy graphics.
- **Clean interface (not obviously AI-generated).** `"hay ciertas cosas que a nivel diseño ya tú dices, 'Okay, esto lo hizo una gente y alguien ah decidió dejar eh ese tipo de cosas.' ... es muy obvio entender cuando un texto fue escrito con así como una interfaz o algún proyecto o algún producto fue hecho con AI."` Use templates, not pure AI generation; show intentional design choices.
- **Demonstrable scalability / real partnership intent.** The reto-4 context (Colsubsidio is the sponsor and will continue development) is explicitly valuable. Judges want to hear: *This is not a throwaway MVP; this solves a real Colsubsidio bottleneck and we're ready to pilot.*

**Penalize**:
- **Self-introduction over problem statement.** Wasted first 20 seconds.
- **Feature lists instead of a use case.** `"¿Qué lo hace distinto? el caso de uso central y no la lista de features."` Judges tune out on bulleted features; they engage on outcomes.
- **Demo failures because you didn't rehearse or back up.** If the live demo fails and you have no backup, judges move on mentally.
- **Inability to explain technical choices** when asked in Q&A. Judges see through copy-paste without understanding.
- **Vague closing or no call-to-action.** A pitch without a "what's next?" feels like theater, not a real project.

## Instructions for finalists — 3-minute jury Q&A

Q&A scenarios she explicitly warns to anticipate:
- `"'¿Y qué harías si te da si en estos tr meses te damos la oportunidad de crear esto?' Ah, eh, no sé. Ah, o ¿qué harías si mañana te poníamos un equipo de backen para construirlo? ¿Cuál sería el stack que que elegirías?"`
- `"¿Cuál es el potencial o tamaño de mercado para este proyecto llevarse a cabo? o busque hasta personas o competidores que ya haya llevado esta idea."`
- Implied: Why would Colsubsidio keep working with you after the hackathon?

Her advice: `"Anticipa preguntas... Hay muchísimas preguntas. Si no se las preguntan de nuevo, aprendieron algo nuevo y lo más importante es que pueden argumentar porque hacen lo que hacen."` The meta-skill: **be able to defend every choice as intentional, not accidental.**

## Demo recording mechanics

Since the submission platform uses **pre-recorded video** (confirmed by Sandra mid-talk):
- Screen record your key workflow (don't try to show the whole product; show only what proves your claim).
- Use CapCut, Instagram, or your phone's native tools to overlay voice narration.
- Keep the narration **short and energetic** — mirror how Ángela presents: punchy, forward-moving.
- Have a **backup MP4 file** in case the live submission system fails.
- For live finals (if you advance), practice the exact same flow 10+ times with a stopwatch so you can deliver it flawlessly under stress.

## General hackathon best practices (meta-level, applicable to our reto execution)

Ángela positions hackathons as **simulations of real work** with a heavy emphasis on **team alignment and morale**, not just code:

- **Connect with mentors from day one.** We have expert access (Colsubsidio staff, 30X organizers, Gemini Live mentor from prior charla, etc.). Use them.
- **Align the team on expectations and roles before writing code.** `"Luego alineen al equipo antes de escribir código... definan roles, que entiendan cuáles son sus escenarios esperados y por supuesto que desde el principio elijan quién va a presentar."` Who presents? Who builds backend/frontend? Who handles design? Who leads the pitch rehearsal? Clarity here prevents 11pm panic.
- **Define three "scope scenarios"** to avoid over-commitment:
  - *Minimum viable*: one button that works (e.g., fuzzy-match a single article in the inventory catalog).
  - *If time permits*: a clean UI around that button.
  - *If time permits*: add advanced features (anomaly flagging, blind-count validation, etc.).
  This prevents the trap of building "Facebook in a weekend" and crashing on Sunday morning.
- **Document as you go** (commit messages, README, architecture diagrams). This both helps the pitcher understand what was built and makes the code-review phase faster for judges.
- **Guard team energy.** `"Cuiden la energía, turnos, descanso, comida... cuando hay poco sueño y no hay buen cuidado de la energía, de nuevo, las emociones salen a flote."` This matters during final pitch prep.
- **Post-hackathon, publish your learnings.** `"A mí genuinamente me gusta como dejar una especie de track record o ah o reporte o recordatorio de las cosas que he ido haciendo... muestren el proceso más allá de los premios. La gente conecta muchísimo con esto."` If you win, write a public post (LinkedIn, etc.) about what you learned. This compounds the networking value.

## Mapped to our reto-4 story and constraints

### The 2-minute pitch structure for our Reto 4 solution:

**Hook (20 sec):** Start with the blind-count error story.
> *"In Piscilago park's monthly inventory, a 'líder de costos' must retype 1405 stock rows manually. A single handwriting misread—9 confused with 90, 3 with 5—costs thousands. And it takes 2 days."* Data: *48 bodegas, 936 distinct articles, monthly blind count.*

**What we built (20 sec):**
> *"We built a tablet app that captures the count in real-time, flags anomalies before they're saved, and asks the counter to confirm or re-count. Blind-count rule is never broken—the app never reveals expected quantities. Result: digitization in hours, not 2 days, zero entry errors."*

**Live demo (50 sec):**
> Show the core workflow on screen: counter says "9 cajas de harina", app voice-confirms "¿9 cajas?", counter taps confirm, anomaly flag triggers (e.g., "this is 5x yesterday's average"), counter re-counts and says "90 cajas", app accepts, demo ends. Show one workflow, not ten.

**Why it matters for Colsubsidio (10 sec):**
> *"Offline-first design works in dead zones. Function-calling LLM backs up fuzzy matching. Auditors see a full trace. Compliance = zero manual retypings at risk."*

**Team + next steps (20 sec):**
> *"Built by [names + roles]. If we win, we pilot with the Piscilago team this month. If it works there, we scale to all 48 bodegas."*

### Why the "wow" factor works for us:
Ángela's advice: start with the problem, not yourself. For Reto 4: the problem is *real, measured, and visible* (9→90 errors, 2 days of rework, audit bottleneck). If we open the pitch with that data and follow with a clean 50-second demo showing the counter being freed from re-confirming after a flag (human-in-the-loop at work), judges will be locked in. The "wow" is not flashy; it's **credible, scoped, and solvable.**

### Finalists Q&A prep (if we advance):
- *"What if you had an extra 3 months?"* → Pilot with Piscilago, gather real anomaly data, retrain fuzzy-match model on actual inventory language.
- *"Why tablet + offline instead of cloud?"* → Colsubsidio's policy: no personal phones, tablets only; network is unreliable in warehouses; offline means counter never loses data mid-entry.
- *"Who competes with this?"* → No direct competitors in blind-count + anomaly-flagging for this vertical in Colombia; SAP/Oracle are overkill and don't fit offline + voice.
- *"How do you validate the fuzzy match is not biasing the audit?"* → Human confirms every anomaly before save; logs are auditable; we're augmenting, not replacing, the counter's judgment.

### Backup demo & rehearsal:
Record a 50-second screen capture of the live demo workflow *in advance*. If the Sunday live demo fails (WiFi, tablet issue, etc.), you submit the MP4. Rehearse the live version 5+ times with stopwatch, ideally with a mentor as the "jury" asking Q&A interruptions so you learn to defend on the fly.

## Caveats & unaddressed tensions

- **The pitch formula assumes a *working* demo is ready by Sunday morning.** Ángela's advice is about *presentation*, not build feasibility. If core voice-to-fuzzy-match is still buggy, no pitch formula will save you. The 2-minute frame compounds time pressure.
- **The "explain how you solved it" bar is high for a hackathon.** We have 48 hours to build blind-count logic, voice capture plumbing, fuzzy matching against a 936-item catalog, offline sync, and anomaly rules. Judges will ask "why did you choose fuzzy match over embeddings?" or "how does your anomaly threshold adapt per bodega?" at 11:59pm Sunday, having built it at 11:30pm Saturday. Being able to **articulate the trade-off** (speed of build vs. precision of match) is more realistic than claiming we've solved it perfectly.
- **The "wow" factor is subjective.** Ángela frames it as "structure + preparation + explanation," which is true, but the reto-4 blind-count narrative is already compelling if told well. Overloading the pitch with technical detail to sound impressive is the opposite of what she recommends: clarity and focus win.

## Verdict: actionable 2-minute pitch skeleton for reto-4

1. **[0–20s] Hook:** "Piscilago park, 1405 stock rows, 48 bodegas, monthly blind count. One mis-read digit costs thousands. And it takes 2 days to fix in the system because a 'líder de costos' retypes it all by hand. We solved that."
2. **[20–40s] What we built:** "A tablet app: counter speaks the count, app voice-confirms, flags anomalies (e.g., '5x yesterday'), counter decides to re-count or confirm. Blind rule intact. Digitization in hours, zero retypings at risk."
3. **[40–90s] Live demo:** Counter says "9 cajas de harina", app confirms and flags, counter re-speaks "90 cajas", app accepts. Show one flow, clean.
4. **[90–100s] Why it matters:** "Offline, voice-backed-by-LLM, auditable, Colsubsidio-policy-compliant."
5. **[100–120s] Team + next:** "[Names]. Pilot Piscilago this month if we win. Scale to all 48."

**Tone:** Energetic, problem-first, human at the center of the loop (the counter is making the decision, the app is assisting). No feature lists. No self-intro.
